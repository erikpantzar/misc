The shift

The three jobs of an AI builder

Produce

Decide what problem to solve and why. Own the outcome from idea to shipped.

Instruct

Give the AI context, constraints and a clear way to verify the work.

Bring taste

Judge what's good. Push back, cut scope, and say no when it isn't right.

The frontier

Most of the work is tinkering

Try

A new model, prompt or tool on a real task.

Measure

Did it save time? Is the result better?

Adjust

Change the instructions, the context or the model.

Refine

Keep what works and make it your new default.

Then a new model arrives, and you go round again.

How you get there

Instructions that work

Goal

“Fix the wrong order date on the confirmation page.”

Context

“It only happens for users in some time zones.”

Constraints

“Keep the change small. Don't touch the emails.”

Verification

“Write a failing test first, then make it pass.”

How you get there

Think like a trail boss

Treat output as a draft

You own the result. Read it, check it, and edit before it goes anywhere.

Iterate, don't restart

Say what's off and ask for a revision. The second answer is usually better.

Let it ask you

End with “ask me anything you need first.” It surfaces gaps you missed.

Appendix · Further reading

What is an LLM?

Trained on huge amounts of text to predict the next piece of text.

It works in tokens, and the context window is its working memory.

Underneath: a big file of numbers plus a small program that runs them.

Read more: https://erikpantzar.github.io/misc/work-in-progress/reading.html#llm

Appendix · Further reading

How a model is made

Pretraining: read a huge pile of text, learn to predict the next word.

Post-training: fine-tuning on examples, people ranking answers (RLHF), Constitutional AI.

The data stops at a date: the knowledge cutoff.

Read more: https://erikpantzar.github.io/misc/work-in-progress/reading.html#training

Appendix · Further reading

Weights, and why sizes differ

Weights are the numbers a model learns in training.

More parameters, data and compute make better models, but bigger costs more to run.

Smaller models via distillation. Open-weight models you can run yourself.

Read more: https://erikpantzar.github.io/misc/work-in-progress/reading.html#size

Appendix · Further reading

Haiku, Sonnet, Opus, Fable

Haiku is fast and cheap, Sonnet balanced, Opus for long hard work, Fable for the hardest reasoning.

They differ in speed, price per token and context window.

Start with Opus, or start cheap and move up. Test on your own tasks.

Read more: https://erikpantzar.github.io/misc/work-in-progress/reading.html#models
