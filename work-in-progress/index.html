Chapter one · The shift

I used to write the code. Now I decide what gets built, and make sure it's right.

The work moved from typing solutions to producing them: defining problems, directing AI, and judging the result.

The shift

How the role changed

I work withMy editorSeveral AI agents at once
My value isHow fast I buildWhat I choose to build, and taste

The shift

The three jobs of an AI builder

Produce

Decide what problem to solve and why. Own the outcome from idea to shipped.

Instruct

Give the AI context, constraints and a clear way to verify the work.

Bring taste

Judge what's good. Push back, cut scope, and say no when it isn't right.

Chapter two · The frontier

The frontier keeps moving

New models

Every few months, a new one that's better at something.

New tools

Agents, integrations and features that didn't exist last year.

New ways to work

What worked six months ago may already be the slow way.

Nobody has it all figured out. That's what makes it exciting.

The frontier

Most of the work is tinkering

Try

A new model, prompt or tool on a real task.

Measure

Did it save time? Is the result better?

Adjust

Change the instructions, the context or the model.

Refine

Keep what works and make it your new default.

Then a new model arrives, and you go round again.

The frontier

Every model has its own temperament

Quick hands

Small, fast models for drafts, sorting and summaries.

Talk to them: short and direct.

Deep thinkers

Larger reasoning models for planning, hard bugs and big decisions.

Talk to them: full context, room to think.

Specialists

Image, voice, search and code tools that do one job well.

Talk to them: in their own format.

Chapter five · How you get there

Never worked with AI? You can saddle up today.

The tools get easier with every release. Start now, and you can be as productive as anyone who started years ago.

How you get there

Start small, then ride further

1

Start small

One tool, one real task. Nothing fancy.

2

Feel it out

Try things, notice what works, and adjust.

3

Grow confidence

Take on bigger problems as your trust builds.

How you get there

Instructions that work

Goal

“Fix the wrong order date on the confirmation page.”

Context

“It only happens for users in some time zones.”

Constraints

“Keep the change small. Don't touch the emails.”

Verification

“Write a failing test first, then make it pass.”

How you get there

Think like a trail boss

Treat output as a draft

You own the result. Read it, check it, and edit before it goes anywhere.

Iterate, don't restart

Say what's off and ask for a revision. The second answer is usually better.

Let it ask you

End with “ask me anything you need first.” It surfaces gaps you missed.

The open range

The only fence out here is your imagination.

What you can build is limited only by what you can picture. So keep exploring, and keep learning.
